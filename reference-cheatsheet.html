2Production Gotchas Missing from gemma.ai

  • NF4 QLoRA degrades audio projections: Use bfloat16 LoRA (with gradient checkpointing) when fine-tuning Gemma 4 audio inputs, or selective quantization in Unsloth.
  • MoE routing drift on 26B-A4B: Never unfreeze the router on small datasets (causes expert collapse), and prefer BF16 Split-LoRA over 4-bit NF4.
  • Silent loss-masking bugs: Always mask system turns, user turns, pad tokens, and audio soft tokens ({256000, 258881, 258883}) to -100 so the model only trains on assistant responses.
  • ClippableLinear & use_cache quirks: Pin transformers>=5.10.1, trl>=0.16.0, peft>=0.19.0 and avoid DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 with LoRA adapters (can save zero-element tensors; use FSDP2 instead).
  • vLLM / SGLang silently ignoring adapters: Merge LoRA weights (merge_and_unload()) and verify greedy outputs match your PEFT checkpoint 1-for-1 before serving at scale.
  • Use official QAT checkpoints for 4-bit serving: -qat-w4a16-ct (vLLM/SGLang) and -qat-q4_0-gguf (llama.cpp), paired with -assistant MTP drafters for lossless speculative decoding.

5Telephony Audio Cheat Numbers

  • PSTN phone line: 8,000 Hz mono, G.711 μ-law (8-bit companded) — cuts off everything above 3.4 kHz. Always test your STT and cloned voice after an 8 kHz round-trip!
  • Gemma 4 & most STT input: 16,000 Hz mono float32 PCM.
  • Modern streaming TTS output: 24,000 Hz mono PCM (downsample cleanly to 8 kHz before sending back over Twilio/SIP).
  • Latency rules of thumb: Human reply gap ≈ 200 ms · Noticeable lag ≈ 800 ms · Target barge-in stop-and-flush ≤ 200 ms.
